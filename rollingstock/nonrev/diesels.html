Center Cab Diesels

From the late 1970s when Metro was under construction, WMATA aquired a set of five 44/45-ton Center Cab Diesel Electric locomotives, mostly from US Army/Air Force surplus. Four were General Electric built, two 44-ton switchers and two 45-ton drop cab switchers. One was a Davenport built 44-ton switcher. All five were regauged to Metro rails and heavily modified to fit under Metro's narrow tunnel clearances. They would be slowly retired over the following decades, with the final one, L002, being sold in 2023.

L001

A General Electric 44-Ton Switcher likely built for the US Army. Its history before WMATA is currently unknown, but based on its design it may have been built in the mid-1940s. After being aquired by WMATA around the 1980s, it was renumbered 846. It had a black livery with yellow handrails and would be rebuilt with a lowered drop cab to fit Metro's tunnel clearances. Shortly after, it would be renumbered again to L001 and gain red/white/blue stripes along its front and rear ends. After 2007, it would be left to decay in Greenbelt Yard along with L011, before being scrapped around late 2010.

L002

A General Electric 44-Ton Switcher built in February 1953 for the US Air Force, originally numbered USAF 1238 (GE #31876). It was stationed at Laughlin Air Force Base in Del Rio, TX. In 1974, it was sold or donated to WMATA for the construction of Metrorail, being renumbered 865. It was rebuilt in October 1974, with a lowered drop cab and an all-yellow livery, with a black Metro logo and number on its cab sides. It was later renumbered again to L002. By 2014, it would be repainted into a new livery, with a white base and blue wing patterns on each end. Its number and "Washington Metropolitan Area Transit Authority" were painted in blue on its sides, along with a white Metro logo on its front and rear vents. It would be auctioned off and sold to Ray's Transportation in New Windsor, NY in 2023. It kept most of its existing livery, with new "Ray's Transportation - New Windsor" text in black.

L011

A General Electric 45-Ton Drop Cab built in October 1944 for the US Army Transportation Corps, European Theater of Operations, originally numbered 8569 (GE #27707). It was shipped to France for World War II on November 13th, 1944 and returned to the US in May 1945. Later would be transferred to Spokane Air Force Base (Now Fairchild AFB) and kept its number as USAF 8569. Transferred again to the US Navy, where it became USN 65-00527 at St. Julien's Creek Naval Annex in VA. It would be sold or donated to WMATA by 1976 for the construction of Metrorail. It gained an even lower drop cab, was renumbered to 860, and was painted in a white livery with orange railings and red/white/blue stripes along the cab and front/rear of the locomotive. By the 1980s, it was renumbered again to L011. After 2007, it would be left to decay in Greenbelt Yard along with L001, before being scrapped around late 2010.

L012

A General Electric 45-Ton Drop Cab built for the US Army Transportation Corps, European Theater of Operations. Its original US Army number is unknown, so its history before WMATA is currently lost. Once aquired by WMATA, it gained an even lower drop cab, was renumbered L012 and was painted in a yellow livery, with a black Metro logo and number on its cab sides. It would be scrapped at an unknown date after 1995.

L021

A Davenport 44-Ton Switcher built in June 1953 for the General Services Administration, originally numbered 1221 (Davenport #3369). It would be aquired by WMATA at some point before mid-1980s and was renumbered 854. It gained a drop cab and had a navy blue livery with yellow, later orange, highlights and railings. Later renumbered again to L021. It would be scrapped after 2006.

Metro Track Constructors

These locomotives were never directly owned by WMATA, but rather a contractor operating as Metro Track Constructors. They were used between 1972 and 1976 on early Metro construction work.

Metro Track Constructors 1

A GE 45-Ton Switcher. It would be operated in Washington DC by Metro Track Constructors as MTC 1 beginning around 1972. It was originally painted yellow with "MTC 1" text on the side. By April 1974, it had recieved a drop cab and was repainted in white with red/white/blue stripes along the top. The number was no longer visible. It would lead the first press train from Rhode Island Ave on January 19th, 1975, pulling a caboose and a flatbed. It's status after leaving the Metro system is unknown.

Metro Track Constructors 9

A GE 45-Ton Switcher built in April 1947 for the Springfield State Hospital, originally numbered SSH 3 (GE #28366). Later, operated in Washington DC by Metro Track Constructors as MTC 9 beginning around 1972. It was painted blue, with "Metro Track Constructors 9" text being added later in yellow. At some point after July 1973, it recieved a drop cab. Following service with MTC, it was sold to Amherst Industries in Huntington, WV by 1982 and renumbered 97. It recieved a black livery with yellow highlights and "Amherst Industries 97" in yellow on it's side. After that, it seemingly moved between various companies, with it's final owners and fate unknown.

Metro Track Constructors 61

A GE 80-Ton Switcher built in September 1943 for the US Army, originally numbered 7873 (GE #18023). Later, operated in Washington DC by Metro Track Constructors as MTC 61 beginning around 1974. It was painted gray, with yellow handrails and "MTC 61" text. In order to fit in Metro's tunnels, it's cab was completely removed, with multiple other panels being removed for ease of maintenance. By 1976, it was titled "The Fumagater" and had both of it's engines nearly completely exposed. It was likely scrapped shortly after 1976.
