Feeler Car

The Feeler Car, also known as the Clearance Car, was used to measure tunnel and track clearances around the Metro system. It would be functionally replaced by the Track Geometry Vehicle.

1028

The pair 1028-1029 would be involved in the January 13th, 1982 derailment between Federal Triangle and Smithsonian. Car 1029 would be destroyed and scrapped in the tunnel, leaving car 1028 orphaned without a mate. It would be modified into the Feeler Car around the late 1980s. It was no longer operable without a pair, so its direction was switched, allowing it to be moved by a diesel or prime mover. The typical brown bulkhead became the rear end and the gray end became the front. On its new front end it gained a headlight above the door and three sets of feelers, front, middle, and rear. Since it was never rehabilitated it retained original vinyl rollsigns, with the front-right displaying "Special" and rear-left displaying "No Passengers". The rear bulkhead door was switched to tan and the rear-right Metro logo was removed or covered for unknown reasons. Car 1028 would be retired and scrapped on April 5th, 2016.

Other Feeler Cars

Prior to the conversion of 1028 to permanent Feeler Car in the late 80s, other 1K cars temporarily served as the Feeler Car when needed. The first test train pair to cross the Yellow Line bridge on August 18th, 1982, featured an unknown feeler car.
