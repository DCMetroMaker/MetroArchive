About

The 1000-Series were the first Metro railcar built for the system's opening, and the only railcars built by Rohr Industries. They were designed by Sundberg-Ferar, who had built a mockup railcar in 1968. The 1Ks had many unique features such as mylar rollsigns, larger bulkhead windows, and two destination signs per side.

Construction

On May 3rd, 1972, WMATA awarded a $91.6 million contract to Rohr Industries to construct and deliver the first 300 Metro railcars. These cars would be known as the 1000-Series and were constructed at Rohr’s plant in Winder, Georgia. The pilot pair, 1000-1001, were delivered by railroad to WMATA’s Brentwood Yard on November 16th, 1974. The 1Ks first entered service on Metro’s opening day, March 27th, 1976, with around 40 of the 300 cars delivered at the time. were the only Metro cars to feature mylar rollsigns, which proved to be unreliable. By 1979 were cut down from five to only three rollsigns per railcar (front bulkhead, front right, rear left) with the others being removed. Between 1985-1986, all rollsigns would be removed from the 1Ks, replacing each car's three rollsigns with the more reliable flip-dot signage used on the 2000-Series.

Rehabilitation

Around the time WMATA ordered the 4000-Series railcars from Breda, they also contracted Breda to mechanically rehabilitate all 298 remaining 1000-Series railcars as they approached their mid-life benchmark. After Breda finished building the 4Ks, the 1Ks would be shipped by truck to a warehouse in Harrison, NJ to be rebuilt. At the same time, WMATA would handle rehabilitating the interior, gutting all seats and installing new carpeting & paneling.

Retirement

As the 1K's reliability continued to decline entering the 2010s, many were pulled from service early and sidelined prior to their official retirement. Their replacement, the 7000-Series, were introduced in 2015. It would be announced in June 2017, that all 1K (and 4K) railcars would be retired on July 1st, 2017, after 41 years of service. The 1Ks would be scrapped at United Iron & Metals in Baltimore, MD. Scrapping began on Feruary 9th, 2016, with car 1013, and would continue until at least September 2017.

Present Day

When the 1000-Series were retired in 2017, WMATA made the decision to retain the first pair of railcars, 1000-1001, for historical significance. In addition, ten 1000-Series cars were donated or sold to other mock subway systems to be used for emergency training simulations.
