Construction

The 4000-Series were initially purchased from Breda as a 68 car order for $87.5 million on April 27th, 1989. At some point later, an option of 32 cars was added, bringing the total 4K order to 100 cars. Between the 2000-Series, 3000-Series, & 4000-Series orders, WMATA had a total of 466 railcars from Breda. The 4Ks were built at a warehouse in Harrison, NJ. From there, they were delivered by truck to WMATA. They entered service in 1992.

Retirement

The 4Ks were originally planned to be rehabilitated in the late 2010s, with plans for them to be compatible with the 7000-Series. However, it was announced in June 2017, that all 4K (and 1K) railcars would be retired on July 1st, 2017, after 25 years of service. Scrapping would take place at United Iron & Metals in Baltimore, MD, beginning on February 15th, 2017, with car 4054, and ending on February 13th, 2018, with car 4008.

Present Day

Following their retirement, multiple 4K pairs were donated for saftey training uses and survive to the present day. Cars 4000-4001 were retained by WMATA as part of their preservation fleet and are stored in Greenbelt Yard. Cars 4020-4021 were donated to the Montgomery County Public Safety Training Academy, where they sit on a short recreation of Metro track for training. Cars 4044-4045 were donated to the Loudoun County Fire and Rescue Training Academy for their Metrorail Training Simulator in preparation for the opening of the Silver Line extension to Loudoun County. Cars 4074-4075 were donated to the New York State Preparedness Training Center. Car 4089 was donated to a redevelopment organization to be used in pilot pop-up shop outside Grosvenor-Strathmore station.
