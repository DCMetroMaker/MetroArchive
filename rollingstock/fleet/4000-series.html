WMATA 4000-Series

Built by: Breda Costruzioni Ferroviarie

Cars Ordered: 100

Cars in Service: 0

Numbered: 4000-4099

In Service: 1992 - June 30th, 2017

Replacement: 7000-Series

About

On May 3rd, 1972, WMATA awarded a $91.6 million contract to Rohr Industries to construct and deliver the first 300 Metro railcars. These cars would be known as the 1000-Series (1Ks) and were constructed at Rohr’s plant in Winder, Georgia. The pilot pair (1000-1001) was delivered by railroad to WMATA’s Brentwood Yard on November 16th, 1974. The 1000-Series first entered service on Metro’s opening day, March 27th, 1976, with around 40 of the 300 cars delivered at the time. Between 1985-1986, Metro removed the colored vinyl rollsigns from all 1000-Series railcars, replacing them with the more reliable flip-dot signage used on the 3000-Series. As the railcars approached their 20th year of service in 1996, Breda Costruzioni Ferroviarie was contracted to rehabilitate all 298 1000-Series railcars. Each car was shipped by truck to a warehouse in Harrison, New Jersey for rehabilitation, with additional interior work being done at WMATA’s shops. Following the introduction of their replacement with the 7000-Series in 2015, all remaining 1000-Series railcars would be retired from service on June 30th, 2017, after over 41 years of service.

Construction

The 4000-Series were initially purchased from Breda as a 68 car order for $87.5 million on April 27th, 1989. At some point later, another option of 32 cars was added, bringing the total order to 100 cars. The 4Ks were built at a Supor's warehouse in Harrison, NJ. From there, they were delivered by truck to WMATA. They entered service in 1992.

Retirement

It was announced in June 2017, that all 4K (and 1K) railcars would be retired on July 1st, 2017, after 25 years of service. Scrapping would take place at United Iron & Metals in Baltimore, MD, beginning on February 15th, 2017, with car 4054, and ending on February 13th, 2018, with car 4008.

Present Day

Following their retirement multiple 4K pairs were donated for saftey training uses and survive to the present day. Cars 4000-4001 were retained by WMATA as part of their preservation fleet and are stored in Greenbelt Yard. Cars 4020-4021 were donated to the Montgomery County Public Safety Training Academy, where they sit on a short recreation of Metro track for training. Cars 4044-4045 were donated to the Loudoun County Fire and Rescue Training Academy for their Metrorail Training Simulator in preparation for the opening of the Silver Line extension to Loudoun County, which would open in 2022. Cars 4074-4075 were donated to the New York State Preparedness Training Center. Car 4089 was donated to a redevelopment organization to be used in pilot pop-up shop outside Grosvenor-Strathmore station.
