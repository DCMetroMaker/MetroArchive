WMATA 5000-Series

Built by: Construcciones y Auxiliar de Ferrocarriles

Cars Ordered: 192

Numbered: 5000-5191

In Service: August 23rd, 2001 - October 12th, 2018

Replacement: 7000-Series

TBD Following the delivery of the 1000-Series order, WMATA awarded the next $75.3 million contract to Breda Costruzioni Ferroviarie on July 12th, 1979 to construct and deliver 94 new railcars. Out of the 94, 76 would be the 2000-Series, while the final 18 would become part of the 3000-Series order. The first pair (2000-2001) was built at Breda’s plant in Pistoia, Italy and delivered to the Port of Baltimore on February 16th, 1983. From there it would be transported to WMATA’s Alexandria Yard for final assembly and was unveiled at a press event on May 24th, 1983. The remainder of the order would be shipped from Pistoia as “car kits” which would be assembled at Amtrak’s Beech Grove Shops in Indiana. The first 2000-Series cars would enter service along with the opening of the Yellow Line extension to Huntington on December 17, 1983. After almost 20 years of service by 2001, Alstom Transportation was contracted to rehabilitate all 364 2K & 3K railcars. The 2Ks underwent their mid-life rehabilitation first at Alstom’s plant in Hornell, New York, where they were rebuilt to be nearly identical to the 3Ks. All 76 2K cars would be rehabilitated by 2004. Years later, the 2Ks would officially be retired on May 10th, 2024.

About

On May 3rd, 1972, WMATA awarded a $91.6 million contract to Rohr Industries to construct and deliver the first 300 Metro railcars. These cars would be known as the 1000-Series (1Ks) and were constructed at Rohr’s plant in Winder, Georgia. The pilot pair (1000-1001) was delivered by railroad to WMATA’s Brentwood Yard on November 16th, 1974. The 1000-Series first entered service on Metro’s opening day, March 27th, 1976, with around 40 of the 300 cars delivered at the time. Between 1985-1986, Metro removed the colored vinyl rollsigns from all 1000-Series railcars, replacing them with the more reliable flip-dot signage used on the 2000-Series. As the railcars approached their 20th year of service in 1996, Breda Costruzioni Ferroviarie was contracted to rehabilitate all 298 1000-Series railcars. Each car was shipped by truck to a warehouse in Harrison, New Jersey for rehabilitation, with additional interior work being done at WMATA’s shops. Following the introduction of their replacement with the 7000-Series in 2015, all remaining 1000-Series railcars would be retired from service on June 30th, 2017, after over 41 years of service.

Retirement

Due to dropping ridership during the COVID-19 Pandemic, all 2000-Series cars were placed into long-term storage in 2020. They would return to service after all 7000-Series railcars were pulled from service on XXXX. By 2024, all 7Ks were back in service, so most 2Ks were quietly moved into storage starting around March 2024.

Present Day

After the bulk of the 2000-Series fleet was retired and scrapped by 2025, only 2 pairs survived. The first pair, 2000-2001, was saved for historical preservation, as WMATA retains the first pair of each railcar series once they are retired. They last ran as part of the 2K Last Ride train on October 19th, 2024 and as of 2026, are currently in storage at Greenbelt Yard, alongside the previously retired 4000-4001 & 5000-5001. Even in storage, 2000-2001 still feature the 2K Last Ride partial wrap & decals. The second pair, 2016-2017, still operate in non-revenue service and have been part of the system's Money Train since 2018, alongside a 6K pair 6182-6183. It is currently unknown how long this pair will be retained in non-revenue service, but if you want to catch a 2K still running in the Metro system, keep an eye out for the Money Train!
