WMATA 3000-Series

Built by: Breda Costruzioni Ferroviarie

Rehabilitated by: Alstom Transportation

Cars Ordered: 290

Numbered: 3000-3291

In Service: December 15th, 1984 - Present

Replacement: 8000-Series

TO BE ADDED On May 3rd, 1972, WMATA awarded a $91.6 million contract to Rohr Industries to construct and deliver the first 300 Metro railcars. These cars would be known as the 1000-Series (1Ks) and were constructed at Rohr’s plant in Winder, Georgia. The pilot pair (1000-1001) was delivered by railroad to WMATA’s Brentwood Yard on November 16th, 1974. The 1000-Series first entered service on Metro’s opening day, March 27th, 1976, with around 40 of the 300 cars delivered at the time. Between 1985-1986, Metro removed the colored vinyl rollsigns from all 1000-Series railcars, replacing them with the more reliable flip-dot signage used on the 3000-Series. As the railcars approached their 20th year of service in 1996, Breda Costruzioni Ferroviarie was contracted to rehabilitate all 298 1000-Series railcars. Each car was shipped by truck to a warehouse in Harrison, New Jersey for rehabilitation, with additional interior work being done at WMATA’s shops. Following the introduction of their replacement with the 7000-Series in 2015, all remaining 1000-Series railcars would be retired from service on June 30th, 2017, after over 41 years of service.

Present Day

After the bulk of the 3000-Series fleet was retired and scrapped by 2025, only 2 pairs survived. The first pair, 3000-2001, was saved for historical preservation, as WMATA retains the first pair of each railcar series once they are retired. They last ran as part of the 3K Last Ride train on October 19th, 2024 and as of 2026, are currently on storage track 4 at Greenbelt Yard, alongside the previously retired 4000-4001 & 5000-5001. Even in storage, 3000-2001 still feature the 3K Last Ride partial wrap & decals. The second pair, 2016-2017, still operate in non-revenue service and have been part of the system's Money Train since 2018, alongside a 6K pair 6182-6183. It is currently unknown how long this pair will be retained in non-revenue service, but if you want to catch a 3K still running in the Metro system, keep an eye out for the Money Train!
