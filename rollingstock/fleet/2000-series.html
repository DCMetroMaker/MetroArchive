About

The 2000-Series were the first of three orders of railcars from Breda Costruzioni Ferroviarie. As a result they appear visually identical to the subsequent 3000-Series and similar to the later 4000-Series. Unlike the 1000-Series, the 2K's front windows are more angular and don't extend all the way to the top of the bulkhead. The 2Ks were also the first Metro railcars to feature flip-dot destination signs, instead of the unreliable mylar rollsigns of the 1Ks. They featured a single destination sign per side above the window directly to the left of the center door. Around the mid-1990s, the 2Ks (and 3Ks) gained external speakers above the middle window to the right of the center door. The placement of these features would become mostly standard throughout the rest of the legacy fleet's designs.

Construction

Following the delivery of the 1000-Series order, WMATA awarded the next $75.3 million contract to Breda Costruzioni Ferroviarie on July 12th, 1979 to construct and deliver 94 new railcars. Out of the 94, 76 would be the 2000-Series, while the final 18 would become part of the 3000-Series order. The first pair (2000-2001) was built at Breda’s plant in Pistoia, Italy and delivered to the Port of Baltimore on February 16th, 1983. From there it would be transported to WMATA’s Alexandria Yard by truck for final assembly and was unveiled at a press event on May 24th, 1983. The remainder of the order would be shipped from Pistoia as “car kits” which would be assembled at Amtrak’s Beech Grove Shops in Indiana. Then they would be shipped on flatbed and unloaded using a car jack at the Alexandria Yard lead. The 2Ks would be tested on the unopened Red Line between Van Ness-UDC and Shady Grove. The first 2000-Series cars would debut as an 8-car train at the opening of the Yellow Line extension to Huntington on December 17th, 1983.

Rehabilitation

After almost 20 years of service, Alstom Transportation was contracted on December 15th, 2000 to rehabilitate all 364 2K & 3K railcars. The 2Ks underwent their mid-life rehabilitation at Alstom’s plant in Hornell, New York, with the first rehabilitated pair (2012-2013) being delivered to WMATA on December 23rd, 2002. All 76 2K cars would be rehabilitated by 2004. The rehabilitation would make the 2K & 3K railcars nearly identical mechanically.

Retirement

Due to dropping ridership during the COVID-19 Pandemic, all 2000-Series cars were placed into long-term storage in 2020. The 2Ks would slowly return to service after all 7000-Series railcars had been pulled from service in October 2021. By 2024, most 7Ks had returned to service and could now operate as 6-car trains, cutting down the total amount of railcars neccessary to run full service. This resulted in the 2Ks being sidelined once again, with most quietly moved into storage starting around March 2024. On May 9th, 2024, it was announced that the 2000-Series fleet would be officially retired the following day. A retirement run, known as the "2K Last Ride", took place on October 19th, 2024, featuring cars 2000-2001, 2038-2039, & 2058-2059. Scrapping was contracted to RemacUSA and would take place at a Sparrows Point, MD scapyard, beginning on January 29th, 2025, with car 2069, and ending on October 2nd, with car 2003.

Present Day

After the bulk of the 2000-Series fleet was retired and scrapped by 2025, only 2 pairs remained. Cars 2000-2001, were saved for historical preservation, as WMATA retains the first pair of each railcar series once they are retired. They last ran as part of the 2K Last Ride train on October 19th, 2024 and as of 2026, are currently in storage at Greenbelt Yard, alongside the previously retired 4000-4001 & 5000-5001. Even in storage, 2000-2001 still feature the 2K Last Ride partial wrap & decals. The second pair, 2016-2017, still operate in non-revenue service and have been part of the system's revenue collection operations since 2020, alongside 6K pair 6182-6183.
