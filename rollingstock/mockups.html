Mockups

Prior to the construction of a new railcar series, full-size mockups are constructed to give an idea of what the finalized railcars will look like.

"144" - Pre-1000-Series Mockup (1968)

The first ever full-size Metro car built. It was constructed by Sundberg-Ferar and accepted by WMATA on August 15th, 1968. It would be transported to Washington DC to showcase and promote Metrorail to the public. On September 8th, 1968, it ws unveiled on the National Mall before being moved to the White House Lawn on the 12th, where it was toured by President Lyndon B. Johnson. After that, it would be exhibited across the DMV at malls and shopping centers including Prince George's Plaza, Marlow Heights, Eastover, Tysons Corner, Landmark, Seven Corners, Turnpike & Pickett, Virginia Square, Belair, Broadale, and Wheaton Plaza. Later in 1968, it was part of the mockup station model at the future site of Rhode Island Ave Station. Its final documented public appearance was Metro's groundbreaking ceremony at Judiciary Sq on December 9th, 1969.

2000-Series Mockup (1981)

This mockup appears to have slightly thinner window frames compared to the final 2Ks and likely uses paper to simulate the new flip-dot destination sign displaying 'ROCKVILLE'. A single image survives of this mockup from a Breda advertisement.

7000-Series Soft Mockup (2011)

A plywood mockup of a 7K railcar built by WMATA to showcase and test the new electronic design aspects. It was constructed out of plywood and painted gray on the exterior. It would be revealed on July 7th, 2011, along with new 7K renderings and information.

"7000M" - 7000-Series Hard Mockup (2012)

This 7K mockup was built by Kawasaki in Kobe, Japan and would be unveiled at the Carmen E. Turner Facility on October 10th, 2012. The design was near finalized, but it featured very minor cosmetic differences from the 7K design including using all capital letters on electronic signage and a different Metro logo on the exterior. It would later be sent to Kawasaki's Lincoln, NE plant during the 7Ks construction, but was returned to the Carmen E. Turner Facility by 2023. It would be renumbered 7000M, given dummy trucks, and is currently used as an Emergency Training Simulator.

8000-Series Soft Mockup (2024)

The first mockup of the 8K in its original blue, black, and silver livery. The mockup consists of two cars within the footprint of a single car (75 feet) in order to showcase the open gangway between the two cars. The A-Car is the front two-thirds, while the B-Car is the last one-third. The mockup was publically displayed on the National Mall in April 2024, before being sent to Hitachi's Factory in Hagerstown, MD. Following the 8K design vote, the mockup was wrapped in the winning brown and silver livery.
